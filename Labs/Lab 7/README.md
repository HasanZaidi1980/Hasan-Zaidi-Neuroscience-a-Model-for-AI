# Lab 07: Working Memory & Executive Attention
### What I Did
I conducted a series of experiments on "Executive Function," focusing on how systems manage limited information over time. I tested AI responses for "Inattentional Blindness" and the "Cocktail Party Effect"—the ability to focus on one signal while ignoring others. I also compared zero-shot and few-shot learning to see how in-context memory functions as a digital version of working memory.

### What I Learned
This lab taught me that "attention" in AI is a mathematical weight, whereas attention in the brain is a competitive process for metabolic resources. I observed that while AI has a massive "working memory" (context window), it still suffers from versions of human-like blindness when a task becomes too complex or distracting.

### Challenges Faced
Designing a test for the "Cocktail Party Effect" was tricky. It required creating scenarios where the AI had to ignore "loud" but irrelevant data to find a specific "quiet" signal. Finding the threshold where the AI's attention mechanism finally broke down required several iterations of prompt engineering.
